Turn 4 · Two dimensions

Access is not reusability

One line has to fold two questions into one: can I get it? and may I use and share it? Social posts break it — visible to everyone, reusable by no one. Splitting the two gives a plane, and the useful part is that the spectrum you already have is the diagonal of that plane. 4a is the plane, 4b keeps two familiar bands and shows the reordering, 4c morphs one into the other.

Rights to use and share — may I reuse, redistribute and build on it? All rights reserved at the bottom, public domain at the top.
Open data needs both. The bottom-right corner is where most of the confusion lives: everyone can see it, nobody may reuse it.
The four corners
Closed — bottom left. Nobody outside can get it, and nobody may reuse it.
Visible, not reusable — bottom right. Anyone can see it; no rights or format to reuse it.
Reusable, but gated — top left. Open terms, restricted access.
Open — top right. Anyone can access, use and share it.
The original spectrum runs along the diagonal — every position on it still means what it always did.

They differ only in how much of the original peaks-and-beams motif comes back: a full landscape (2a), light beams descending from the band (2b), or a whisper behind everything (2c). The peaks never sit under a label as a container, so they can't be read as buckets.

Turn 1 · Continuum redesign

One continuous spectrum, three reference points

Three treatments of the same structure, each answering your two open questions differently. All are wide web banners (1180 px), all keep every word as live text on its own layer for relocalisation, and all carry a non-colour cue (hairline density) alongside the labels. 1d plots your examples; 1e is the draggable version.
